\section*{Introduction}
This analysis investigates whether there is a statistically significant correlation between year and temperature in the Key West, Florida dataset spanning 1901-2000. A permutation test was conducted to assess the significance of the observed correlation coefficient.

\section*{Methods}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dataset: Key West Annual Mean Temperature (1901-2000)
    \item Observed correlation between Year and Temperature: $r = 0.533$
    \item Permutation approach: 10,000 random shuffles of temperature values
    \item Test statistic: Pearson correlation coefficient
    \item Null hypothesis: No correlation between year and temperature
    \item Alternative hypothesis: Non-zero correlation (two-sided test)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Results}
\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Statistic} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Observed Correlation & 0.533 \\
Mean Permuted Correlation & -0.001 \\
Standard Deviation & 0.101 \\
p-value & 0.000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section*{Interpretation}
The permutation test reveals strong evidence against the null hypothesis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The observed correlation of 0.533 indicates a moderate positive relationship between year and temperature
    \item None of the 10,000 permuted datasets produced a correlation coefficient as extreme as the observed value
    \item The resulting p-value of approximately 0.000 provides strong statistical evidence ($p < 0.001$) that the correlation is not due to random chance
\end{itemize}

This suggests a statistically significant warming trend in Key West, Florida over the 20th century, consistent with broader climate change patterns observed in many regions.
